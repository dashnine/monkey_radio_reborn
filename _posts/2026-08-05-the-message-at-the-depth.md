**Artist:** DJ Krush · **Year:** 2002 · **Rating:** ★★★½

The Message at the Depth is a good album, but it's not what I think of when I think of DJ Krush, particularly in the first half.

<!--more-->

The album launches with water-themed ambient sounds into a breakbeat; similar (shorter) interludes punctuate the album at several points. The first half of the album has a decidedly industrial theme; of particular note is track 2, "Toki no Tabiji (Journey of Time)," which was present on Monkey Radio under the moniker ["Kanji."](/most-wanted/#dj-krush--kanji) Here Krush's scratching accompaniment to the Japanese vocals is almost unrecognizable against the metallic found-sound music and widely varying reverbs.

The heavily experimental and industrial tenor continues until the album pivots on Song for John Walker (track 6):

```
The names of prominent families carry no weight in foreign cities
And even the sons of senators receive no welcome outside the states.
```

The lyrics and melody here still feel experimental, but (unlike, in my opinion, earlier tracks) the experiments work: the song is catchy but unpredictable, and easily the most unusual track on the disk. Afterwards, the album resolves into a much more familiar rhythm.

## The second half

After another interlude we settle into much more predictably "Krush" tracks, with guests providing a sampling of different varieties of triphop against comfortably ominous beats. If there's a criticism of the second half, it's that by the time What About Tomorrow's pop-friendly dub wraps, we've perhaps entered "too safe" territory.  This doesn't stop the other Monkey Radio track, "But the World Moves On," from being a highlight: the melody stands out with impressive, jazzy lines against menacing beats. The track sounds almost like something off of Ki-Oku.

## The verdict

深層 "The Message at the Depth" is a hard album to rate: it features some amazing tracks (and wonderful collaborations) but one rarely is looking for discordant found-sound industrial music when reaching for DJ Krush. It feels like it tells a story, but after a couple listens I'm more likely to pick and choose tracks rather than play it end to end. As such, I'll level a 3.5 star rating on this disk: it's a great album, but not a Krush favorite.

## Monkey Radio Tracks: 2/11
